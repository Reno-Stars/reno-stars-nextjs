The 5 Phases of a Vancouver Whole-Home Renovation

Phase 1: Design, Scope and Permit (1–3 weeks)

Before any work begins, Reno Stars drafts a detailed scope of work and, if required, submits permit applications to the relevant municipality. In Vancouver, a permit for structural changes takes 2–4 weeks depending on the city. Cosmetic work such as painting, flooring, and trim does not require a permit. Reno Stars handles permit applications for every project that needs one, including basement suites in Vancouver where legalizing a secondary suite requires a separate building permit and an occupancy inspection.

Phase 2: Demolition and Site Prep (3 days – 1 week)

Demolition removes existing finishes, cabinetry, or fixtures being replaced. For a whole-home project this can take 3 days in a condo to a full week in a detached house. Reno Stars crews protect adjacent areas with dust barriers and 重物覆盖 and handle all demolition debris removal. A typical Vancouver whole-house renovation demo covers flooring removal, bathroom tile strip-out, and kitchen cabinet removal in a coordinated single-day to three-day window.

Phase 3: Rough-in Trades — Electrical, Plumbing, HVAC (1–3 weeks)

Once demo is complete, the rough-in stage begins. Electricians run new wiring, plumbers install rough pipes, and HVAC contractors modify ductwork if needed. This stage is the most dependent on scheduling and typically takes the longest in a whole-home project. A single-trade project such as a WFH office conversion that only needs electrical work can complete rough-in in one day. A multi-trade whole-house project where electrical, plumbing, and HVAC all need updating can take 2–3 weeks for rough-in alone.

Phase 4: Taping, Sanding, Priming, and Painting (1–2 weeks)

After rough-in passes inspection, the space is insulated, drywall is installed, and finishing begins. Taping and sanding typically takes 3–5 days per coat with drying time between coats. In Metro Vancouver's climate, water-based paints may require additional drying time in winter months. Painting of walls, trim, and ceilings is the most visible phase of the renovation and usually proceeds fastest once the space is ready.

Phase 5: Fixture Installation and Final Walkthrough (3 days – 1 week)

The final phase installs all fixtures — lighting, plumbing hardware, cabinetry, countertops, flooring, and any built-in features. Reno Stars schedules a structured walkthrough with the homeowner before declaring the project complete. Any punch-list items are addressed before the final invoice is issued. Most whole-home projects complete installation and walkthrough within 3–5 days of the finishing phase.

Common Timeline Delays in Vancouver Renovations

Material lead times are the most common source of unexpected delay in 2026. Quartz countertops require 2–3 weeks from template to installation in Metro Vancouver. Imported tile or custom cabinetry can add 4–6 weeks. Reno Stars accounts for these lead times in the initial project schedule and orders long-lead items immediately after scope is finalized to avoid pushing the completion date.

Permit backlogs vary by municipality. Vancouver's building department typically processes residential permits within 2–4 weeks for standard projects, but complex structural work or laneway house applications can take 6–8 weeks. Reno Stars monitors permit status and notifies clients of any delay as soon as it appears.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a whole-home renovation take in Vancouver?

A targeted whole-home project such as a WFH office conversion or a single-bathroom update takes 1–2 weeks. A mid-size project covering multiple rooms such as flooring, painting, and bathroom updates takes 3–5 weeks. Does a whole-home renovation require a permit in Vancouver?

Cosmetic work — painting, flooring, trim, and cabinet installation — does not require a permit. Electrical panel upgrades, plumbing rerouting, structural changes, basement suite conversions, and any work that changes the building's occupancy all require a permit. Reno Stars obtains all required permits before work begins and schedules the required inspections.

For single-room or single-floor projects, most homeowners can remain in the house.
