What Does a Dental Clinic Renovation Include?

Dental clinic renovation differs from residential work in three critical ways. First, Vancouver Coastal Health (VCH) requires compliance with the Health Facilities Design Guidelines — minimum 44-inch door widths for wheelchair access, negative air pressure in surgical rooms, and specific plumbing for chair basins. Second, dental equipment (compressor, X-ray units, autoclave) requires dedicated electrical circuits that most general contractors miss. Third, when the clinic stays open during renovation, work must happen in phases — which doubles labour time.

Permits for Dental Clinic Renovation in Vancouver

Any dental clinic renovation in Vancouver, Burnaby, or Richmond requires a Commercial Building Permit from the municipality. A change of use — for example, converting retail to a dental clinic — triggers a full life-safety upgrade under BC Building Code Part 3, which can add $15,000–$30,000 to the project. Interior alterations only (no change of use) typically require a simplified permit with lower fees.

Key inspections required: structural (if walls are moved), electrical (dedicated circuits for equipment), plumbing (chair waste lines), and HVAC (negative pressure verification for surgical areas). Vancouver Coastal Health may also conduct a pre-opening inspection for facilities providing clinical services.
