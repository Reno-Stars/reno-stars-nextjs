What Is the Difference Between Commercial and Residential Renovation?

A commercial renovation modifies a space where customers, clients, or employees are the primary occupants — retail stores, restaurants, medical offices, warehouses, and childcare facilities. A residential renovation modifies a space where the occupants are the property owner or tenants living there long-term. The difference sounds simple, but it drives every downstream decision: building code classification, permit requirements, construction standards, finish levels, and cost.

The BC Building Code classifies buildings by occupancy type. A retail store occupied by members of the public is a Group C or Group D occupancy. A single-family home is a Group C occupancy. Commercial occupancies trigger different fire-resistance ratings, exiting requirements, accessibility standards, and mechanical specifications than residential occupancies — even when the two buildings sit on the same street in the same Vancouver neighbourhood.

When to Choose a Commercial Renovation

Choose a commercial renovation when the property is classified as a commercial occupancy or when the end use requires public access, customer-facing finishes, or specialized mechanical systems. Examples from our project portfolio include a toy store renovation in Metrotown Burnaby where the retail fit-out required upgraded electrical for display lighting and barrier-free access upgrades to meet City of Burnaby bylaws, and a dental clinic renovation in Burnaby requiring specific infection-control finishes, Type II water supply upgrades, and CPBC compliance documentation. A residential renovation on these spaces would have failed inspections and created liability exposure for the property owner.

Commercial renovations also make sense when the property's income function requires spaces that function differently than a home — open retail floor plans, commercial kitchen hoods, warehouse racking systems, or childcare play areas with specific floor-area ratios. The building code requirements for these uses cannot be satisfied through a residential renovation scope.

When to Choose a Residential Renovation

Choose a residential renovation when the property's primary function is shelter and long-term habitation. A basement suite renovation in Vancouver that converts an unfinished basement into a legal secondary suite, a kitchen-and-bathroom gut renovation in a Richmond townhouse, and a whole-house renovation in Surrey that brings a 1970s home up to current insulation and electrical standards are all residential renovation projects. They require residential building permits, residential-grade finishes, and residential mechanical standards — a commercial contractor quoting these projects is either misclassifying the work or will deliver the wrong scope.

Residential renovations also apply to multi-family buildings with up to four units per floor (Group C occupancy) — which covers most Vancouver duplexes, triplexes, and low-rise apartment buildings. Strata-title units additionally require strata council approval before any renovation commences, which adds 4–8 weeks to the project timeline in our experience across Metro Vancouver strata buildings.

BC Building Permits for Commercial vs. Residential in Vancouver

Commercial occupancies in Vancouver require a commercial building permit for any work that modifies structural elements, changes occupancy type, or alters fire-safety systems. The City of Vancouver, City of Burnaby, and City of Richmond each have published commercial permit checklists. In our experience, a straightforward commercial tenant improvement permit in Vancouver takes 3–6 weeks for first review; complex projects with structural work or change-of-occupancy applications can take 8–14 weeks.

Residential renovation permits vary by scope. A kitchen or bathroom renovation that does not alter structural elements, plumbing rough-ins, or electrical panel capacity typically qualifies for a simplified residential permit or, in many cases, no permit at all for cosmetic work. Moving plumbing fixtures, removing load-bearing walls, or upgrading an electrical panel always requires a permit — commercial or residential. Frequently Asked Questions

Can the same contractor do both commercial and residential renovation?

Yes, if the contractor holds the appropriate licences for both scopes. Reno Stars holds residential builder registration with BC Housing and commercial general contractor registration. Commercial renovation requires coordination with City of Vancouver or Metro Vancouver municipality building departments for occupancy classification and permit review; residential renovation follows the BC Building Code's Part 9 for most single-family and small multi-family projects. Both licences are standard for full-service renovation companies operating across both sectors in Metro Vancouver.

How long does a commercial renovation take compared to a residential renovation?

A retail tenant improvement in Vancouver typically takes 6–12 weeks from permit issuance to handover, depending on scope complexity. A restaurant renovation with Health Authority approval requirements typically takes 10–20 weeks. A mid-range residential kitchen renovation in Vancouver takes 6–10 weeks; a whole-house residential renovation takes 12–24 weeks. Commercial projects tend to have longer permitting timelines because change-of-occupancy applications and public-occupancy fire-safety reviews add stages that residential renovations do not face.

Do I need strata approval for a commercial renovation in Vancouver?

Strata approval applies to residential strata lots, not commercial units in commercially-zoned buildings. If you own a commercial unit in a mixed-use strata building, the building's council may have design-review requirements separate from the municipal permit — but strata approval for renovations in BC applies to the residential portions of strata properties, not to commercial tenants under a separate occupancy classification. Always confirm with the building manager before commencing design work.

What insurance does a commercial renovation contractor need in BC?

A commercial renovation contractor in BC must carry commercial general liability (CGL) insurance appropriate to the project value — we carry up to $5M CGL per occurrence, which is standard for commercial renovation contracts in Metro Vancouver. The property owner's own insurance should also be notified of any renovation work, and for commercial occupancies the tenant should confirm their business-interruption insurance covers the construction period. WorkSafeBC coverage is mandatory for all contractors working in BC regardless of commercial or residential classification.

How do I know if my Vancouver renovation requires a commercial permit vs. a residential permit?

The determining factor is the occupancy classification of the building and the scope of work. If the building is classified as a commercial occupancy (Group D, E, or F in the BC Building Code) or if the work changes the occupancy type (e.g., converting a residential unit to a retail space), a commercial building permit is required.
