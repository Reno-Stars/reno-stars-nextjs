## Content (EN) — full
<p>Homes built in Metro Vancouver between 1975 and 1995 have a hidden problem buried inside their walls: Polybutylene (Poly-B) piping. This cheap, now-infamous plumbing material failed at such scale that it triggered a class-action settlement in Canada worth over $1 billion. If your Vancouver home still has Poly-B pipes, you are sitting on a risk that worsens every year.</p>

<p>Reno Stars has replaced Poly-B systems in homes across Richmond, Vancouver, Burnaby, and Surrey. Here is what that work costs, what it involves, and why acting before a failure event is almost always cheaper than responding to one.</p>

<h2>What Is Poly-B Pipe and Why Does It Fail?</h2>

<p>Polybutylene pipe is a gray or blue plastic tubing installed in roughly 1 in 4 Canadian homes built during the 1970s through mid-1990s. At the time, builders chose it because it was inexpensive and easy to install. The problem is that Poly-B degrades when exposed to oxidised drinking water — a process that happens gradually in every home with municipal water supply.</p>

<p>When the pipe fails, it fails fast. A burst Poly-B fitting can release thousands of litres of water into a home in hours. Unlike a slow leak, a burst gives you no time to call a plumber. The water damage bill routinely exceeds the repipe cost itself.</p>

<h2>What Does the Work Involve?</h2>

<p>A full Poly-B replacement at a minimum involves replacing all hot and cold supply lines from the main shutoff to every fixture group. In most Vancouver homes, this means opening walls in utility areas, bathrooms, and kitchens to access the old piping.</p>

<p>Reno Stars typically completes a standard three-bathroom Richmond home in 7 to 10 working days. Residents can usually remain in the home during work — only the immediate work zone is disrupted, and water is restored each evening. Expect 2 to 4 hours per day without water service while connections are being transferred.</p>

<h2>Partial vs. Full Repipe: When Each Makes Sense</h2>

<p>Not every home needs a complete replacement. A partial repipe targets only the highest-risk sections — typically the hot water run and the main distribution manifold — and can extend the life of an otherwise intact system by 10 to 15 years at roughly one-third the cost of a full replacement.</p>

<h2>Does Home Insurance Cover Poly-B Replacement?</h2>

<p>Most Canadian home insurers will not renew a policy on a home with confirmed Poly-B piping, or they will add a significant exclusion for water damage. Some insurers require a Certificate of Quality (sometimes called a Plumbing Condition Report) before binding coverage on older homes.</p>

<p>The cost of a repipe is frequently compared to the cost of one major water damage claim — which in a Richmond or Vancouver basement renovation context can easily reach $40,000 to $80,000 once structural drying, mould remediation, and flooring replacement are included.</p>

<h2>Poly-B in Specific Metro Vancouver Cities</h2>

<p>Richmond homes, particularly those built in the 1980s near No. 3 Road and Moncton, have among the highest Poly-B concentration of any Metro Vancouver neighbourhood. Surrey and North Vancouver also show heavy concentrations in post-1980 developments. Burnaby's older system (homes built before 1990) should be inspected regardless of apparent condition.</p>

<p>Vancouver's 1970s-and-older character homes occasionally have Poly-B in additions or basement suites that were not part of the original construction.</p>

<h2>FAQ: Poly-B Pipe Replacement in Vancouver</h2>

<h3>How do I know if my Vancouver home has Poly-B pipes?</h3>
<p>Look for gray or blue plastic piping (sometimes black) entering the home from the water meter, near the hot water tank, or accessible under sinks. Poly-B is typically stamped with the markings "PB2110" or "Polybutylene." If your home was built between 1975 and 1995 and you have never had the plumbing replaced, assume Poly-B is present until a licensed plumber confirms otherwise.</p>

<h3>Is Poly-B pipe still being installed in Vancouver?</h3>
<p>No. Poly-B was withdrawn from the Canadian residential market in 1998 following the class-action settlement. All replacements use PEX (cross-linked polyethylene) or copper, both of which carry manufacturer warranties of 25 years or more.</p>

<h3>Can I sell my Vancouver home with Poly-B pipes?</h3>
<p>You can, but most buyers' home inspectors flag Poly-B during condition reviews, and many lenders require a plumbing condition certificate before approving a mortgage on a home with confirmed Poly-B. Failing to disclose known Poly-B issues also creates legal liability in British Columbia.</p>

<h3>How long does a full Poly-B repipe take?</h3>
<p>A typical three-bathroom single-family home in Richmond or Vancouver takes 7 to 12 working days. Condos and townhouses with simpler layouts may be completed in 3 to 5 days.</p>

<h3>Will my home insurance drop me if I have Poly-B?</h3>
<p>Many BC insurers will either decline to renew or add a water damage exclusion for homes with confirmed Poly-B piping. Replacing the piping with PEX and obtaining a plumbing condition certificate from a licensed contractor is the standard path to restoring normal coverage terms.</p>
