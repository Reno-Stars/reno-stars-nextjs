# How Long Does a Kitchen Renovation Take in Vancouver? Realistic Timelines for 2026

A Vancouver kitchen renovation typically takes 6–14 weeks from demolition to final walkthrough, with permits and material lead times as the most common sources of delay. Reno Stars has completed more than 35 kitchen remodels across Metro Vancouver; here is what actually drives the timeline in 2026.

## Why Permits Take 2–4 Weeks in Vancouver

Most kitchen renovations in Vancouver require a building permit under the Vancouver Building Bylaw. A standard non-structural kitchen remodel — replacing cabinets, countertops, flooring, and appliances — usually qualifies as a "minor renovation" and may be processed faster than a major addition. However, any work that alters plumbing, electrical, or structural walls triggers a full permit application that the City of Vancouver processes on a first-come, first-served basis. In 2026, routine kitchen permit applications are taking 10–15 business days for initial review, with a second review round if documents are incomplete. Choosing a contractor who submits complete drawings and coordinated structural-engineer letters on the first submission is the single most effective way to keep this phase on schedule.

## The Five Phases of a Vancouver Kitchen Renovation

Reno Stars organizes every kitchen project into five distinct phases, each with a typical duration:

- **Phase 1 — Design and selections:** 1–2 weeks. Includes measurements, layout revisions, and finishes selection (cabinet style, countertop material, tile, hardware).
- **Phase 2 — Permit application and approval:** 2–4 weeks. Depends on scope; cosmetic-only renovations may not require a permit at all.
- **Phase 3 — Demolition and rough-in:** 3–7 days. Existing cabinets, flooring, and backing are removed. Plumbing and electrical rough-in happens concurrently.
- **Phase 4 — Installation and finishing:** 3–5 weeks. New cabinets, countertops, backsplash, flooring, lighting, and appliances are installed. Paint and trim work happens in parallel.
- **Phase 5 — Final inspection and walkthrough:** 3–5 days. City of Vancouver inspectors review any permitted work. Punch-list items are completed.

**Total estimated timeline: 6–14 weeks** from signed contract to keys returned to the homeowner.

## What Can Extend the Timeline?

Material availability is the leading cause of schedule slippage in 2026. Imported engineered quartz countertops and custom-coloured cabinetry from certain suppliers now carry 4–8 week lead times, compared to 2–3 weeks a year ago. Reno Stars sources locally stocked materials whenever possible to avoid these delays. Structural surprises — rot in wall studs, outdated wiring, or unpermitted prior work — are the second most common cause of delays and are typically discovered during demolition. A thorough pre-renovation inspection of accessible areas reduces this risk.

## Comparing Vancouver Neighbourhood Timelines

Permit timelines are consistent across the City of Vancouver, but neighbourhood-specific factors can affect scheduling. Older homes in Kitsilano, Dunbar, and Kerrisdale frequently require updated electrical panels, which adds both cost and time. Homes in new developments in Marpole or River District follow a more predictable renovation schedule because wiring and plumbing are already to current code.

## How Much Does a Vancouver Kitchen Renovation Cost in 2026?

Budget and timeline are closely linked. A cosmetic refresh — painting, refacing cabinet fronts, replacing hardware, and new flooring — typically costs $15,000–$35,000 and may not require a permit. A mid-range full replacement of cabinets, countertops, appliances, and flooring runs $40,000–$75,000. A premium custom kitchen with structural changes, high-end appliances, and designer finishes can exceed $120,000. Each tier adds time as well as cost: custom cabinetry alone requires 3–6 weeks of fabrication and delivery.

## FAQ: Kitchen Renovation Timelines in Vancouver

**Do I need a permit for a kitchen renovation in Vancouver?**

You need a permit if your renovation moves plumbing, alters electrical circuits, or modifies structural walls. Cosmetic work — painting, replacing cabinet doors, installing new flooring — generally does not require a permit. The City of Vancouver provides a pre-application consultation service for projects that fall in a grey area.

**How long does a City of Vancouver kitchen permit take in 2026?**

The City of Vancouver's target processing time for straightforward kitchen renovation permits is 10–15 business days. Complex applications or those requiring additional reviews can take 4–6 weeks. Incomplete submissions trigger a second review cycle that adds 2–3 weeks.

**What is the biggest cause of kitchen renovation delays in Vancouver?**

Material lead times have surpassed permit delays as the primary schedule risk in 2026. Custom countertops and specialty cabinet colours are the most common culprits. Ordering materials early in the design phase — before permits are submitted — is the most effective mitigation.

**Can I live in my home during a kitchen renovation?**

Yes. Reno Stars works sequentially, completing demolition and rough-in before installation, which minimizes disruption to daily routine. The kitchen is typically unusable for 2–3 weeks during the most intensive phases, but homeowners can remain in residence throughout.

**Does kitchen renovation size affect timeline?**

Linearly. A galley kitchen in a strata unit may complete in 5–7 weeks. A large open-concept kitchen with an island, custom cabinetry, and high-end finishes can require 10–14 weeks. Scope is the primary timeline driver, not square footage alone.
