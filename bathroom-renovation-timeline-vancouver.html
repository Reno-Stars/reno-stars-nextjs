How Long Does a Bathroom Renovation Take in Vancouver? Real Timelines From 2026 Projects

A bathroom renovation in Vancouver typically runs 3 to 8 weeks, depending on scope. A basic refresh — new vanity, tile, toilet, and fixture swap — takes 3–4 weeks. A mid-range renovation with a new shower enclosure and waterproofing runs 4–5 weeks. A full gut with layout changes, curbless shower, and plumbing relocation can stretch to 6–8 weeks. This guide is based on real Reno Stars projects completed in Metro Vancouver in 2026.

How Scope Drives Your Timeline

Every bathroom renovation timeline has four phases that run sequentially regardless of property type: demolition (3–5 days), rough-in plumbing and electrical (3–7 days), tiling and waterproofing (5–10 days), and finishing (3–7 days). Adding a layout change or moving plumbing adds 1–2 weeks to rough-in. Strata approval for condos in Vancouver or Richmond adds 4–8 weeks before work can start.

Phase-by-Phase Bathroom Renovation Timeline

  • Week 1 — Demolition: Remove existing tile, vanity, toilet, and fixtures. Check for water damage or mold behind walls. This phase typically runs 3–5 days for a standard bathroom.
  • Week 1–2 — Rough-in plumbing and electrical: Move drain lines and water supply if layout is changing. Install new electrical circuits for heated floors, vanity lighting, or exhaust fans. This adds 3–7 days for a layout change.
  • Week 2–3 — Waterproofing and tiling: Install Schluter waterproofing membrane on all wet walls. Tile floors and shower enclosure. This is the longest single phase at 5–10 days depending on tile complexity and bathroom size.
  • Week 3–4 — Finishing: Install vanity, toilet, fixtures, mirror, and accessories. Grout and seal. Final cleanup and inspection. This phase runs 3–7 days.

What Vancouver Building Rules Add to the Timeline

Any renovation in Vancouver that changes plumbing require a permit from the City of Vancouver. A straightforward vanity-and-fixture swap does not need a plumbing permit, but moving a toilet or shower drain does. Permit processing in Vancouver takes 5–10 business days for straightforward jobs; 3–4 weeks for anything involving structural changes or multi-family buildings. Your contractor should pull the permit — Reno Stars includes permit acquisition in the project scope for Vancouver clients.

In Richmond and Burnaby, strata approval is required for any work that affects the building's common elements or exterior appearance. Condo owners should budget an additional 4–8 weeks for strata approval before demolition can begin. Strata approval timelines in Vancouver proper are typically shorter for owner-occupied units.

What Can Delay a Bathroom Renovation

  • Strata delays: The single most common delay for Metro Vancouver condos. Apply early, in writing, with detailed scope description and drawings.
  • Special order tiles or fixtures: Imported or non-stock items can take 3–6 weeks to arrive. Order during the demolition phase to stay on schedule.
  • Asbestos in buildings built before 1990: If your building was constructed before 1990, floor tile glue may contain asbestos. Testing is required before demolition; abatement adds $1,500–$5,000 and 1–2 weeks.
  • Plumbing surprises in older homes: Vancouver homes built before 1970 often have galvanized steel pipes that need replacement during a bathroom renovation. This can add $2,000–$6,000 and 3–5 days.

Bathroom Renovation Timeline FAQ

How long does a small bathroom renovation take in Vancouver?

A small or powder room refresh — new vanity, toilet, fixtures, and floor tile — typically takes 2–3 weeks from demolition to final clean. This assumes no layout changes and no strata approval needed. Reno Stars has completed powder room renovations in Richmond in as little as 1 week for a basic fixture swap.

How long does a full bathroom renovation take in Vancouver?

A full bathroom gut renovation with new tile, shower enclosure, vanity, plumbing fixtures, and bathroom flooring takes 4–6 weeks in Metro Vancouver. Adding a layout change or moving plumbing fixtures adds 1–2 weeks. A multi-bathroom renovation scales roughly linearly: two bathrooms typically run 5–7 weeks, three bathrooms 7–10 weeks.

Does a bathroom renovation require a permit in Vancouver?

A Vancouver building permit is required when the renovation involves moving plumbing fixtures, altering structural elements, or changing the use of a room. Simply replacing a vanity, toilet, and fixtures without moving drains does not require a plumbing permit. Changing a tub to a shower or moving a toilet requires a permit. Permit fees in Vancouver range from $500 to $2,000 and processing takes 5 business days to 3 weeks depending on complexity.

What is the longest phase of a bathroom renovation?

Tile and waterproofing is typically the longest phase at 5–10 days. Large-format tile, a curbless shower, or a feature wall with mosaic each add time. The waterproofing step (Schluter membrane or equivalent) cannot be rushed — it requires curing time between coats. Rushing this phase is the most common cause of callbacks and water damage claims.

How do I speed up my bathroom renovation timeline?

Three actions cut timeline: order special items (tile, fixtures, vanity) during demolition rather than after, get strata approval submitted before demolition begins, and choose a layout that does not move plumbing. Choosing a pre-fabricated shower enclosure over a custom-tiled walk-in saves 3–5 days. Selecting in-stock tile and fixtures versus special-order items can cut 2–4 weeks from the overall schedule.
